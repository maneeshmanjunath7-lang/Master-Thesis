\subsection{Central Node fraction and forwarding policy}

Figure~\ref{fig:final_cn_response} shows that the central-node response depends on the forwarding policy. Under the bounded nominal policy, mean observation peaks near an intermediate fraction and falls sharply at high fractions. Under the unlimited useful-deadline diagnostic, dissemination continues to improve toward CN100 while communication demand rises.

For the bounded policy, the CN90-to-CN100 observation change is $-21.93$ percentage points in California and $-21.43$ in India. The decline establishes a package--policy interaction but not a unique mechanism: eligibility, forwarding preference, deterministic placement, and the link-budget multiplier change together. Under peer routing relative to central-only routing, observation changes by $+5.53$ points in California and $+4.70$ in India, while strict completion changes by $+11.68$ and $+13.58$ points. The corresponding traffic increases are approximately 1,061 and 19,347 Mbit per case.

\subsection{Packet-size sensitivity, timing, and robustness}

Packet growth has a much larger effect in the dense India workload. Doubling packet size reduces strict completion by 3.18 points in California and 15.36 in India. Quadrupling packet size reduces it by 14.94 and 37.02 points. These are paired within-case contrasts and therefore isolate scenario changes within the declared architecture and task population.

The censor-aware analysis keeps missed events as right-censored observations and reports results by the fixed priority horizons of 30, 60, 180, and 360 minutes. At CN40 under the unlimited diagnostic, equal weighting of the shared High, Medium, and Low classes gives observation attainment/speed of 0.81/0.50 for California and 0.77/0.43 for India. For strict completion, the corresponding values are 0.82/0.51 and 0.71/0.32. Stratification prevents the much larger Low-priority population from silently defining the event-window comparison.

At 30\% severity, outage of the sole Munich ground station causes the largest individual mean strict-completion loss: $-20.35$ points in California and $-17.50$ in India. This is operationally important because removing a ground-contact interval blocks injection of new tasks for all spacecraft, whereas a random satellite or link loss removes only part of the available temporal graph. Figure~\ref{fig:final_robustness} shows this separation: the ground-station curve declines most strongly, while the node, link, and capacity disturbances retain a larger share of their matched baselines over the evaluated range. The result identifies a single-point vulnerability in the tested network, but it does not quantify the benefit, location, or cost of an additional station. Fourfold packets combined with 20\% satellite failure reduce strict completion by 17.86 points in California and 42.01 in India. The India loss is more than a minor perturbation: the denser workload consumes the remaining transfer opportunities after nodes are removed, so isolated failure tests understate the risk of simultaneous load growth and component loss.

\section{India 180-Satellite Results and Fleet-Scale Extension}

\subsection{Central-node response in the balanced block}

Figure~\ref{fig:t180_cn_response} covers the balanced CN30--CN100 block. With bounded routing, mean observation decreases from 88.15\% at CN30 to 43.52\% at CN100. Strict completion moves from 0.25\% to zero, and useful coverage from 11.20\% to 2.04\%. This is more than a flat, saturated response: the implemented package performs worse when nearly every spacecraft receives the Central Node role. The curve cannot identify one congestion mechanism because placement, preference, link eligibility, link budget, hop limit, and copy limit are changed together. It does establish that the role cannot be assigned to the whole fleet without changing the routing behaviour.

The unlimited diagnostic moves in the opposite direction. From CN30 to CN100, observation rises from 90.07\% to 92.36\%, strict completion from 88.98\% to 97.16\%, and useful coverage from 95.74\% to 99.17\%. Mean P100 $T_{100}$ falls from 257.08 to 142.03 minutes. The contacts created at high participation are therefore useful, but the bounded forwarding rules do not exploit them effectively. This gain also requires traffic: mean transferred data increases from 45,476 to 105,760 Mbit per case. A 133\% traffic increase buys 2.29 points of observation and 8.18 points of strict completion. CN100 is attractive only if that faster, broader completion is worth the added communication burden.

\subsection{Policy and packet-size effects}

Peer and hybrid policies are again nearly identical. Relative to central-only routing, each improves observation by 0.28 points and strict completion by approximately 0.94 points, but adds about 29,300 Mbit per case. The smaller marginal mission change at 180 satellites does not mean the policies are free: the traffic difference is substantial.

Halving packet size improves observation by 1.05 points and strict completion by 2.59 points. Doubling packet size reduces them by 6.13 and 10.49 points. Quadrupling packet size reduces observation by 22.32 points and strict completion by 33.67 points while adding about 133,628 Mbit per case. Removing the bounded forwarding and copy limits changes observation by $+11.98$ points and strict completion by $+94.92$ points, with about 71,546 Mbit additional traffic. Figure~\ref{fig:t180_scenario_effects} summarizes these paired effects.

\subsection{Failure robustness and combined stress}

At each family's maximum evaluated severity of 30\%, mean strict-completion retention is 76.26\% for ground-station outage, 93.99\% for targeted Central Node failure, 95.28\% for random Central Node failure, 95.43\% for capacity degradation, 95.47\% for link-window outage, and 95.67\% for random satellite failure. Figure~\ref{fig:t180_robustness} shows that the ground-station series separates progressively from the other failure families as severity increases; the result is therefore a pattern across the tested levels rather than a conclusion based only on the 30\% endpoint. Ground access is the dominant tested single vulnerability in the sensitivity experiment as well. Targeted Central Node loss is worse than random Central Node loss, as expected when the removed nodes are selected for structural importance, but the difference is small compared with the ground-station effect. The evidence supports prioritizing a ground-network trade study before fine optimization of failure-aware Central Node placement. It does not yet support a specific number or location of additional stations.

\section{Integrated Result Synthesis}

The regional and 180-satellite blocks of the final campaign support a consistent system-level interpretation. Central Nodes create useful temporal paths, but their value depends on forwarding constraints and workload. Mission gains can saturate while traffic continues to grow. Packet size is a strong design driver, especially for the India load case. Ground access is the largest tested single vulnerability across the campaign, while combined load and failure produce losses that isolated disturbances do not reveal. Exact Pareto and rank-acceptability results support portfolios rather than universal winners.

The final campaign identifies a controlled India 120-to-180 effect for the 216 matched CN30--CN100 cells: the larger fleet improves mission completion and timing, but requires about 25.8~Gbit more task-message traffic per case. Low-CN 180-satellite coverage is incomplete, and no California 180-satellite grid exists. The result is therefore a qualified India scale comparison, not a universal three-fleet or cross-region law.
