\section{Conclusions}

This thesis developed and evaluated a communication-constrained workflow for disseminating time-critical wildfire follow-up tasks through a distributed satellite system. The completed work connects remote-sensing-derived task generation, Walker constellation design, temporal communication, Central Nodes, reception-before-observation logic, explicit failure replay, and multi-objective post-processing.

The final verified simulation campaign is treated as one body of evidence. It contains 594 matched California and 594 matched India cases at 60 and 120 satellites, together with 282 verified India cases at 180 satellites. Every retained case follows the same 539-scenario contract. The complete CN30--CN100 part of the 180-satellite extension contains 216 cases and supports a direct comparison with the corresponding India 120-satellite architectures. This structure allows all final results to be discussed together while keeping each comparison within the data actually executed.

On matched California and India architectures, the India workload has 4.66 percentage points higher observation fulfilment but 2.98 points lower strict useful completion, 5.95 points lower useful-recipient coverage, approximately 175 minutes longer worst-case complete dissemination, and 0.04 lower robustness-retention AUC. These simultaneous changes establish that observation, dissemination completeness, timing, and communication burden cannot be represented by one metric.

Central Nodes are useful, but more are not always better. With bounded routing, high participation can sharply reduce observation. When copy and hop limits are removed, completion improves toward higher fractions while traffic rises. Peer-capable routing improves strict completion relative to Central Node-only routing. Larger packets are particularly harmful for the India workload. The outage of the sole ground station is the largest individual vulnerability tested, and combined packet growth with satellite failure produces losses that are not visible in nominal performance alone.

The 180-satellite results strengthen the same campaign-level interpretation. From CN30 to CN100, unlimited strict completion rises from 88.98\% to 97.16\% and P100 $T_{100}$ falls from 257.08 to 142.03 minutes, while traffic rises from 45,476 to 105,760 Mbit per case. Fourfold packets reduce strict completion by 33.67 points. Ground-station outage retains $76.26\pm1.48$\% of baseline strict completion across base architectures at 30\% severity, whereas fourfold packets combined with 20\% satellite failure retain 57.49\%. The 61-case exact Pareto set and the maximum rank-1 acceptability of 26.55\% confirm that the larger-fleet choice also depends on mission preferences. Recomputed two-objective fronts contain between 1 and 10 cases, showing that a simple trade plot can be more selective than the full decision problem but cannot replace it.

Across 216 common India architectures under unlimited routing, moving from 120 to 180 satellites increases observation fulfilment by 1.23 points, strict completion by 3.99 points, and useful-recipient coverage by 1.55 points. Worst-case complete dissemination is 44.68 minutes faster, but task traffic increases by 25,827 Mbit per case. The larger fleet therefore improves the tested mission outcomes without dominating the resource objective.

Taken together, the results reveal why no single architecture dominates the tested objectives. The final engineering output is one tiered architecture portfolio: cross-window candidates supported in both event windows, supplemented by large-fleet candidates whose evidence applies to the India workload. The distinction records comparison scope; it does not represent the 180-satellite results as a different or provisional simulation.

\section{Answers to the Research Questions}

\paragraph{RQ1: How do Central Node fraction and forwarding policy change timely task dissemination and follow-up observation in the implemented distributed satellite system?} The two factors act together. Intermediate fractions work best with the bounded package, while the unlimited diagnostic continues to benefit from higher fractions and also produces more traffic. Since eligibility, forwarding preference, placement, and the link-budget boost are changed as one package, the evidence supports the complete implemented treatment rather than an isolated component effect.

\paragraph{RQ2: How do workload, packet size, constellation geometry, and injected failures change performance and the relative ranking of architectures?} Each factor changes the result values and, in several comparisons, the architecture order. On matched 60/120-satellite cases, India has slightly higher observation fulfilment but weaker, later, and less robust complete dissemination. Fourfold packets, ground-station outage, and combined packet/failure stress produce the largest changes. Geometry changes the temporal opportunities, but no factor level is best in every tested context.

\paragraph{RQ3: Which architectures provide defensible trade-offs among observation fulfilment, deadline-complete dissemination, communication burden, resource proxies, and robustness?} Exact Pareto filtering and weight sensitivity identify a five-case cross-window core and 61 Pareto-efficient 180-satellite extensions. Together they form a tiered portfolio rather than competing reports. A final choice still requires declared mission priorities, the workload to be served, and an agreed set of preference weights.

\section{Scientific and Engineering Contributions}

The completed thesis work comprises:

\begin{enumerate}
\item conversion of Sentinel-3 fire detections into traceable satellite tasks with priorities and deadlines;
\item implementation of a temporal simulation in which physical access, communication, task reception, and follow-up observation remain separate events;
\item definition and verification of a 539-scenario contract, including not-applicable states, deterministic de-duplication, and task-population auditing;
\item priority-dependent time analysis with censoring instead of discarding unfinished tasks;
\item paired evidence for workload, routing, packet size, failures, and combined stress;
\item redundancy checks, exact Pareto filtering, and rank acceptability for the architecture decision; and
\item demonstration that a valid restart must check input hashes, regional and priority counts, and index normalization in addition to completed scenario identifiers.
\end{enumerate}

These contributions provide a documented path from an input task to a reported claim, retain unsuccessful outcomes, and distinguish a technically completed job from a scientifically comparable simulation.

\section{Recommended Architecture Decision}

The portfolio begins with the 60-satellite candidates that are supported in both event windows. \path{T060_P10_H0800_I0986_CN040_F1} combines strong California rank stability with relatively low India traffic. The 60-satellite CN90/CN100 alternatives at 1000~km and 98.6-degree inclination place greater emphasis on India completion. The 120-satellite CN100 design is faster for India, but is justified only when that improvement warrants roughly twice the traffic of the comparable 60-satellite CN100 case.

For an India workload where a 180-satellite fleet is feasible, \path{T180_P10_H0500_I0986_CN050_F1} is the first large-fleet portfolio member to inspect. It has 26.55\% rank-1 acceptability under the random family-weight analysis, completes the reported mission metrics, and uses less traffic than the faster CN90/CN100 alternatives. Its evidence applies to the balanced 773-task India block and is therefore reported alongside, rather than substituted for, the cross-window candidates.

Raw mission, timing, traffic, fleet, Central Node, and robustness values should be compared before approved preferences are applied. The weights used here are analyst-defined scenarios, not elicited stakeholder consensus. Likewise, the risk-screen likelihood categories are declared engineering assumptions rather than simulated probabilities. The sole-ground-station result motivates a ground-network trade study but does not quantify the benefit or location of a specific additional station.

\section{Limits and Future Work}

The completed campaign establishes an India 120-to-180 effect on the 216 matched CN30--CN100 architectures, but it does not establish an unrestricted 60-to-120-to-180 law. The 180-satellite coverage is unbalanced below CN30, and a matched California 180-satellite population would be required for a large-fleet event-window contrast.

The next research steps are:

\begin{enumerate}
\item complete the 15 missing India 180-satellite low-CN cells and execute a matched California 180-satellite grid using the verified task-hash and positional-index contract;
\item repeat representative cases for 500, 700, and 900~km observation radii to quantify the sensitivity of opportunity and recipient definitions;
\item separate Central Node eligibility, forwarding preference, placement, and link-budget enhancement in a component-ablation design;
\item compare balanced, random, and graph-centrality-based Central Node placement using several seeds;
\item evaluate two or more ground-station networks and explicit outage correlation, because the one-station baseline is the dominant tested vulnerability;
\item vary packet construction, task clustering, priority thresholds, and deadline rules to test whether the packet-size effect survives plausible wildfire-product definitions;
\item retain trace-level queue, forwarding, and dropped-copy decisions for diagnosing the bounded high-CN decline;
\item extend random-failure and combined-stress trials until mean, fifth-percentile, retention-AUC, and rank-acceptability estimates meet predefined convergence thresholds;
\item add cloud, smoke, instrument pointing, imaging duration, image downlink, and alert-delivery models to extend the endpoint beyond a geometric follow-up opportunity;
\item sample additional seasons and wildfire event windows before making geographic or climatological generalizations;
\item elicit stakeholder weights and feasibility thresholds from mission operators rather than treating analyst-defined weights as preferences; and
\item replace the present satellite/Central Node/traffic proxies with lifecycle cost, power, mass, and operations estimates for an implementable mission trade study.
\end{enumerate}

Within the analysed evidence, routing participation, task workload, forwarding limits, packet size, constellation geometry, and failure conditions jointly determine whether information delivery becomes a deadline-complete follow-up opportunity. A transparent portfolio with explicit evidence scope is therefore more defensible than a universal Central Node fraction or fleet-size recommendation.
